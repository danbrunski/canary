### What the drive settled

On the operator's pulls, PID 0x44 reported commanded **φ 1.195–1.242** and PID 0x24 measured **1/λ 1.13–1.28**, agreeing to ~1–2 % across two windows (26 and 79 rich samples). Memory corrected.
2. **The injector-PW mass-balance constant is ~15 % low at WOT and must not be used there.** With command and measurement agreeing at φ ≈ 1.21, the PW+MAF arithmetic says φ ≈ 1.03. The 914,943 counts/gram constant was fit in closed loop below 0.034 g/injection; these samples are ~0.040 g. Part of the gap is `ECM_MAF_GPS` reading ~5 % low at high flow (1:1 vs Mode 01 MAF: slope 0.934, r² 0.909, `drifting`). Re-fitting needs open-loop samples — this drive has them.
3. **The rich-side instrument dispute has its number.** `afr_calibration.yaml` said it needed one sustained WOT pull on this profile; it got two. The UpRev-derived voltage transfer reads **+3.14 % (n=140, AFR 12.34) to +3.71 % (n=102, AFR 12.11)** lean of the ECM's own λ, while agreeing to **−0.02 %** at stoich (n=3,584). That is the same size and sign as the historical −3.84 % UpRev-vs-Pi bias, which suggests the campaign's "engine runs 3.9 % lean of commanded" was largely the conversion, not the engine. UpRev's own logger remains the unlogged fourth path.
4. **DID 0x1134 is the best target-AFR candidate this project has had** — and still a candidate. It holds 64 through 98 % of a 30-minute survey and leaves it only under load (26 of 37 excursions in the top-5 % throttle regime, none in decel). Decoded as AFR = 14.7 × 64 / raw it is the ROM Fuel Targets convention at half scale, reads 12.54–12.71 AFR where the ROM cells for that rpm hold 12.30–12.63, and the wideband falls monotonically as it rises (2.145 V at baseline → 1.210 V at 75, both banks). Every check is within one instrument or across two drives, so it needs one capture holding 0x1134 and PID 0x44 together.
5. **MCP2515 overflow tracks burstiness, not average request rate** (D02.2). ### D02.2: MCP2515 overflow tracks *burstiness*, not average request rate
Block 8 was a deliberate control for block 6: the same wire rate, evenly paced instead of fired in rounds. `can0 rx_over_errors` from the kernel counter (`/sys/class/net/can0/statistics`), `rx_dropped` flat at 0 throughout, broadcast traffic ~1,640 frames/s in every case:

| condition | our request rate | pacing | overflows/min |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| `default` profile | 8 req/s | rounds (up to 22 channels fired together) | 0.4 |
| `tuning-did` | 127 req/s | one DID at a time, continuous | 0.05 |
| `did_log` (block 3b) | 118 req/s | one at a time, 6 ms gap | 1.5 |
| **`tuning-hybrid-research` (block 6)** | **47 req/s** | **Mode 01 rounds + DID rounds, 0.25 s cycle** | **5.2** |
| **`did_log` paced (block 8)** | **45.8 req/s** | **one at a time, 19.5 ms gap** | **1.7** |
| `did_log` unthrottled (block 3a) | ~650 req/s | back-to-back | 36 |

The two 46–47 req/s rows are the controlled pair: **bursty is 3× worse than paced at the same average rate.** And paced 118 req/s (1.5/min) is no worse than paced 46 req/s (1.7/min), so our average diagnostic rate barely matters when requests are spread out. The 650 req/s case is consistent — at 1.5 ms spacing it *is* a continuous burst.

Mechanism this supports: the MCP2515 has **two** hardware RX buffers, so what overflows them is several frames arriving faster than the SPI driver drains them, regardless of the long-run average. A round of 8–11 requests answered back-to-back is exactly that; the same requests spread over the same second are not.

**Caveat, and it matters:** broadcast traffic and driving differed between these windows, and each row is one window, so treat the ordering as a lead rather than a calibrated law. But the controlled pair (block 6 vs block 8) was 40 minutes apart on the same road at the same rate, which is the comparison D02.2 asks for.

**What this says about D02.2's two candidate fixes:** raising `spimaxfrequency` from the overlay's 10 MHz default attacks the drain side and stays worth trying — it needs a reboot, so it is a desk/driveway change, not a mid-drive one. But there is now a **third** candidate that needs no hardware change at all: **stagger the scheduler's round instead of firing it as a burst.** The requests already fit the budget; they just arrive together. That is a software change in the OBD scheduler, and today's pair is the evidence for it.
