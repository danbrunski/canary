Method: `~/canary-data/scripts-0922/d036_measure.py`. Each stage in its own
subprocess so `ru_maxrss` is that stage's own peak; config
`canary.example.yaml`; laptop (15 GB, 12 cores). Sessions are the real pulled
ones; the child stages ran on a temp directory of symlinked chunks so no
`digest.json` was written into `~/canary-data`.

|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---|

| same | | `compute_digest` in-process | 959 MB | 3.5 s | SessionDigest |
| same | | `compute_digest_under_limit`, RLIMIT_AS 4096 MiB | **976 MB** | 3.6 s | completed |
| same | | `handle_trip_end_off_loop`, default budget | — | 0.0 s | **refused** `over_row_budget` (1,952,257 at 2,200 B/row) |

| 2026-09-18T144215 | 5,570,352 | `resample_and_segment` | **1,470 MB** | 6.9 s | 2,359 rows × 135 cols |
| same | | `compute_digest_under_limit`, 4096 MiB | 1,490 MB | 6.3 s | completed |

Against the 2026-09-17 figure on the same 2,622,370-row session — **5,483 MB
peak** for the raw-timestamp pivot — the reduction is **6.4×** (859 MB), and
the whole digest under the Pi's 4 GiB ceiling completes at 976 MB. Per row:
**390 B** (2.6 M rows) and **280 B** (5.6 M rows); the old constant was 2,200.
`RUSAGE_CHILDREN` read 0 for the spawn-pool child (it is not reaped through
`wait4` by the caller), so the child's cost is taken from the in-process run
under the same `RLIMIT_AS`, which is the same code path.

**Budget revised from this evidence:** `DIGEST_PEAK_BYTES_PER_ROW` 2,200 →
**800** (twice the larger laptop measurement, because the Pi's allocator is
not the laptop's). At the Pi's 4 GiB child that is **5,368,709 rows ≈ 38 min**
of `default` collection at ~2,355 rows/s, up from 1.95 M rows ≈ 13.6 min. A
wrong revision fails as a caught `MemoryError` under the ceiling and a
`digest_skipped.json`, never the daemon. Both sessions above would now be
digested at trip end instead of refused.

**Shutdown:** a digest child cancelled by `_settle_trip_end_tasks` now writes
`digest_skipped.json` with reason `interrupted_at_shutdown` and is remembered
process-locally, so the shutdown fallback's second call for the same session
is a no-op instead of a second multi-gigabyte child with the full 900 s
timeout inside `TimeoutStopSec=30`
(`tests/test_trip_end_digest.py::test_a_digest_cancelled_at_shutdown_is_recorded_and_not_restarted`).
Not done: the fallback in `src/daemon/runtime.py` still passes the 900 s
timeout for a *different* session than the one cancelled; that file is held by
another session today.

## D04.7 — VVT duty scale bound from saturation

Max over every `did_log` window on disk (3a, 3b, 5, paced47):

| register | 3a | 3b | 5 | paced47 | ≥ 200 | plateau |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| 0x1110 | 130 | 149 | **190** | 153 | never | none (one sample at each max) |
| 0x1139 | 221 | **255** | 154 | 146 | 5 samples | **3 consecutive samples at 255** (3b) |

- **0x1139 exceeds 200** (221, and pins at 255 for three consecutive samples):
  if it is a percent register, ×0.5 % is ruled out for it; a plateau at the
  byte limit is consistent with ×100/255 at 100 %, or with an 8-bit quantity
  that is not a percent at all.
- **0x1110 never reached 200** in any window, so saturation gives it **no
  bound**: ×0.5 % and ×100/255 % both remain possible. It did not plateau.
- **The shared-unit premise does not hold.** In block 5's 1,002 passes where
  both are non-zero, 0x1139/0x1110 has p10/p50/p90 = 0.61 / 0.71 / 1.82; at
  0x1139's maximum the same pass read 0x1110 = 0, and at 0x1110's maximum
  (190) 0x1139 read 99. The 0x1139 bound therefore does not transfer to 0x1110.

## D04.8 — barometric pressure against GPS altitude

`~/canary-data/scripts-0922/altitude_d048.py`. Prediction: standard atmosphere
at P0 = 101.325 kPa from a 7 s centred rolling median of `gps:altitude`,
injected as a channel and paired against `obd:BAROMETRIC_PRESSURE` with
`compare_channels`, 1 s tolerance, baro as reference.

**Known answer first** — `2026-09-18T144215` reproduces the 09-18 report
exactly: n_pairs 114, slope 0.910, offset +6.63 kPa, r² 0.866, `drifting`,
free-P0 102.54 kPa with residual 0.00 ± 0.47 kPa.

**`2026-09-18T180630`** (the 9 kPa descent): 2,613 fixes, all fix_quality 1,
3,245 → 2,095 m; baro 79 → 70 kPa (67 samples, one every ~30 s).

| | |
|---|---|
| `compare_channels` | n_pairs **67**, slope **0.825**, offset +13.74 kPa, r² 0.939, **`drifting`** (88 %), b−a +1.08 ± 0.69 kPa, median separation 0.20 s |
| free sea-level-equivalent P0 | **102.81 kPa**, residual +0.02 ± 0.71 kPa; implied P0 p10/50/90 101.58 / 103.11 / 103.97 |
| free-P0 residual by predicted-pressure tercile | **+0.57** (68.0–71.7 kPa), −0.11, **−0.44** (72.9–78.4 kPa) |
| lag scan, baro shifted earlier 0…420 s | monotonically worse: slope 0.825 → 0.822 (30 s) → 0.746 (120 s) → 0.566 (300 s); no lag improves it |

Reading: the day's P0 agrees with the AM session (102.5 vs 102.8 kPa), but the
**slope deficit is real, not a range artefact** — doubling the span from 5 to
9 kPa lowered the slope from 0.91 to 0.83, and the free-P0 residual trends
1.0 kPa across the span. ISA gives 10.6 kPa for 2,095 → 3,245 m; the PID
moved 9. A warmer-than-ISA atmosphere explains at most ~3–8 % (the measured
slope would need a mean air temperature of ~328 K); the PID is a 1 kPa/bit
integer so it cannot carry a scale error; a delayed ECM estimate is excluded
by the lag scan. One of the two instruments is compressed by ~15–20 % over
this descent and CAN alone cannot say which. Nothing goes into
`expectations.yaml`. The next step is a third instrument (a phone barometer
or surveyed elevations along the descent), not a longer single-session climb.

## D04.2 — the decel-fuel-cut boundary, pooled over three captures

`~/canary-data/scripts-0922/coasts_d042.py` over the three 2026-09-18
`fuel-pw-research` captures (T140805, T174742, T200809). Fuel cut = DID 0x1206
at its floor cluster (0.47–0.58 ms; next value above ≥ 1.46 ms), the same
detector as the 09-18 PM report. Throttle closure = the 100 Hz broadcast
`ECM_THROTTLE_SENSOR_V` back within 0.15 V of its closed floor (0.684 V).
Speed is broadcast wheel speed (reads 8–16 % under GPS; ratios are inflated
by the same factor in every gear).

**84 coasts** (11 + 32 + 41); 68 with ≥ 2 floor samples and speed > 5 km/h.

| gear (relative) | rpm/km·h | n | cut rpm (lift) min–median–max | lowest rpm seen at the floor | natural refuels (throttle still closed) | lowest natural-refuel rpm @ km/h |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| A | 28.6 | 24 | 1,912–2,431–2,775 | 1,825 | 12 | 1,800 / 1,925 / 1,950 @ 64–68 |
| B | 36.7 | 22 | 1,662–2,681–3,325 | 1,650 | 15 | 1,337 / 1,600 / 1,712 / 1,737 / 1,787 @ 44–49 |
| C | 49.9 | 13 | 1,900–3,187–3,600 | 1,887 | 9 | 1,887 / 1,900 @ 38 |
| D | 71.4 | 9 | 1,812–2,350–3,225 | 1,462 | 8 | 1,287 / 1,325 / 1,350 / 1,550 / 1,637 @ 19–27 |

- **Onset:** the cut follows throttle closure by **0.4–1.4 s** (cut bracket
  lower bound p10/50/90 = 0.42 / 0.85 / 2.82 s; first floor sample 1.02 /
  1.39 / 3.45 s; n = 65). The cut rpm is whatever rpm the lift happened at,
  1,660–3,600 in every gear: no onset-rpm threshold is visible above ~1,650.
- **Exit:** the lowest closed-throttle refuel rpm **rises with gear** —
  ~1,290–1,640 in D (19–27 km/h), ~1,340–1,790 in B (44–49 km/h),
  ~1,800–1,950 in A (64–68 km/h) — while speed at refuel rises far more. So
  it is **neither purely rpm-gated nor purely speed-gated**; the shape fits a
  speed-indexed recovery-rpm table. Hysteresis (cut − refuel) medians: A 125,
  B 144, C 131, D 262 rpm (p10–p90 8–1,055) — of limited meaning because the
  cut rpm is the lift rpm.
- **Unexplained:** 2,100–3,500 rpm refuels with the throttle still closed on
  long descents (e.g. B: cut 3,112 → refuel 2,387 after 59 s; cut 2,150 →
  2,387 after 37 s with rpm rising). Clutch, brake or a maximum-duration
  rule are all possible; none is on the tags.
- **Still owed (truck):** tagged protocol coasts (in gear, and one in
  neutral) so ECM refuel is separable from driver action; absolute gear
  numbers need the gearbox ratio table, which is not in the vehicle profile.
